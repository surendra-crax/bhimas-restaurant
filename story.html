Our Story

A family's devotion to the flavours of South India — and a dream to share them with the world.

“Food is never just sustenance in our family — it is memory, love, and the thread that binds every generation together.”

Bhimas Traditional Indian Kitchen was born from a simple but powerful conviction: that authentic South Indian cooking deserved a home in Luxembourg. When Rajesh Bhima arrived in Luxembourg City in 2009, he found a vibrant international community — but precious few places where he could find the flavours of his childhood in Kerala.

Rajesh had grown up watching his mother and grandmother cook over wood fires in their ancestral home in Thrissur. The slow reduction of tamarind into a dark, complex rasam. The hiss of mustard seeds hitting hot coconut oil. The patient grinding of fresh coconut with green chillies for a chutney no jar could ever replicate. These were not just recipes — they were rituals.

After three years of cooking for friends and neighbours in his apartment — winning over Luxembourgers, Indians, Portuguese, and Belgians alike — Rajesh made the leap. In November 2012, Bhimas Traditional Indian Kitchen opened its doors at 14 Rue de la Gare, with twelve tables, a clay tandoor oven, and an unwavering commitment to cooking food his grandmother would recognise.

In those early days, Rajesh cooked everything himself, sourcing fresh vegetables from the weekly market in Bonnevoie and importing whole spices — long pepper, Malabar cardamom, true cinnamon — directly from Kerala. The menu was small and focused: a handful of starters, three curries, one biryani, and a dessert of gulab jamun. Every evening, the twelve tables were full.

Word spread through Luxembourg's growing Indian community, then outward into the city at large. Local food writers took notice. Corporate clients began booking the whole restaurant for team celebrations. Bhimas became something rare — a neighbourhood restaurant that felt equally at home hosting a young couple's first date and a minister's retirement dinner.

Today, Bhimas has grown its menu to over 80 dishes, its team to fifteen passionate cooks and front-of-house staff, and its dining room now seats sixty guests. But the cooking philosophy has never changed: every spice is still ground in-house, every sauce is still made from scratch, and every dish still carries the soul of a family kitchen in Kerala.

Rajesh Bhima
Founder & Head Chef, Bhimas Kitchen
Meet the Founder

Rajesh Bhima

Chef, Restaurateur & Keeper of Family Recipes

Rajesh grew up in Thrissur, Kerala, in a household where the kitchen was the heart of daily life. His mother, Lakshmi, cooked every meal from ingredients grown in their courtyard garden — curry leaf trees, drumstick pods, raw mangoes. His grandmother, Kamala, was the keeper of the family's masala blends, recipes she guarded with affectionate secrecy.

Rajesh trained formally at the Institute of Hotel Management in Trivandrum, then spent eight years in professional kitchens across India, the UAE, and the UK before settling in Luxembourg with his family in 2009. The decision to open Bhimas came not from a business plan but from a conversation at a dinner party: a Luxembourgish friend told him that his cooking was "the most alive food I have ever tasted."

That sentence changed everything. Rajesh spent two years perfecting his menu, negotiating suppliers, and converting a former brasserie on Rue de la Gare into a warm, intimate dining room. He insisted on cooking with a clay tandoor — even when his contractor told him it was impossible to install in a Luxembourg listed building. He found a way.

Today Rajesh oversees the kitchen with his son Arjun, who trained in Paris and brings a refined eye to presentation while fiercely guarding the authenticity of every recipe. Together, they are writing the next chapter of a story that began in a Kerala kitchen three generations ago.

— Rajesh Bhima

What We Stand For

Our Values

Three principles guide every decision we make — from choosing our suppliers to training our team.

Heritage

Every recipe on our menu has a history. We follow centuries-old techniques — from dum slow-cooking to stone-ground masalas — because the traditional method is always the best method. We do not modernise for the sake of it; we preserve because these flavours are irreplaceable.

Freshness

We shop daily, prepare daily, and serve daily. Nothing at Bhimas sits in a freezer waiting. Vegetables come from local Luxembourg growers and the market on Rue du Fort Neipperg. Our whole spices arrive from Kerala quarterly and are ground each morning — never more than we need for that day's service.

Community

Bhimas has always been more than a restaurant — it is a gathering place for Luxembourg's diverse community. We host cultural evenings, Diwali celebrations, and cooking workshops open to all. A portion of our annual revenue supports the Luxembourg-India Friendship Society's education fund for children from low-income families.

A Brief History

Milestones

2009

Rajesh Arrives in Luxembourg

Rajesh Bhima relocates to Luxembourg with his family, joining the European Commission as a culinary consultant. He begins cooking South Indian suppers for friends, quickly developing a loyal following.

November 2012

Bhimas Opens Its Doors

The restaurant opens at 14 Rue de la Gare with twelve tables and a menu of twenty dishes. Every table is booked on opening night. The Wort newspaper calls it "Luxembourg's most exciting new restaurant of the year."

2016

Expansion & Tandoor Installation

Bhimas acquires the neighbouring space, doubling capacity to sixty covers. A second clay tandoor oven is installed — a feat of engineering that required reinforcing the floor and specially lining the flue. The tandoor breads programme launches, becoming an instant signature.

2020

Arjun Joins the Kitchen

Rajesh's son Arjun, after training at Le Cordon Bleu Paris and a stage at a Michelin-starred restaurant in Lyon, returns to Luxembourg. He takes on the role of Sous Chef, refining presentation while ensuring every dish remains true to its Kerala roots.

Today

Still Cooking with the Same Soul

More than 80 dishes, 15 staff, and thousands of satisfied guests later — Bhimas remains exactly what it was always meant to be: a place where the cooking is honest, the welcome is warm, and every meal feels like coming home.
